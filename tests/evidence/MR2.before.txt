MR2 — the `App` accessor batch: hiding the application, refreshing its windows,
and the keyboard layout.
Tier: `gos run --main-thread`.

BEFORE THE WORK. The probe as it stood at HEAD, which the ledger had carried as
`implemented` since round 80 with "**the probe has not run**":

    git show HEAD:tests/gui/mr2_hide.gos > examples/zz_mr2_before.gos
    DSH_ALLOW_WINDOWS=1 ./scripts/run-once.sh examples/zz_mr2_before.gos 45

Output:

    window-guard: windows ALLOWED (DSH_ALLOW_WINDOWS=1)
    MR2 the keyboard layout this machine is using is "U.S."
    MR2 called refresh() — no observable, and none claimed
    MR2 called hide() — the whole application should now be off the screen: bring it back from the dock, then press the button
    run-once exit: 124
    wall: 45s

Nothing was wrong with `hide()` or `refresh()` — the two calls are carried out, and
the first two lines are real answers off the platform. What fails is the *instrument*:
the probe asks a person to bring the application back from the dock and press a
button, and there is no person. `exit 124` is the timeout killing it.

**A hidden window gets no more frames, so even the probe's own timeout could never
have fired.** That was measured this round, not assumed: with the application
hidden, the frame after the call arrives within a tick — 34ms and 36ms in two runs —
and then the loop goes quiet. One run's next frame came 23 seconds later; another
run's did not arrive within a further 60. So the `gpui::now() > 120.0` line that was
meant to report "the person did not confirm" was unreachable code, and the probe's
only possible endings were a press or the harness's kill.

That is the row's real obstacle: not the capability, but a check that needed a human
at exactly the moment the window is not on screen for them to press.
