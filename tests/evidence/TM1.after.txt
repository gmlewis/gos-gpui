The verdict carries no numbers on purpose — they are measured, and a verdict that
carries measurements can differ from one tier to the next. What the probe checked,
and what the numbers that were observed are:

  * a width, an em width, a line height and an inherited-size width all positive
  * two copies of a word wider than one
  * **doubling the size doubles the width** — 41.6230583190918px at `fs=20`,
    81.46484375px at `fs=40`, a ratio of 1.957, required to be within
    1.8 to 2.2
  * `W` advances further than `i` — 18.96px against 4.55px
  * `fs=32` is wider than the window's inherited size

None of those is a font's metric, so none of them depends on which font this
machine resolves — which is what makes them a check rather than a transcription.

What makes it pass:

  * `gpui-binding/src/view.rs` — `begin_frame` captures, per window, the window's
    own `Arc<WindowTextSystem>` and the font and size its inherited `text_style()`
    resolves to. `text_width`, `text_em_width`, `text_advance` and
    `text_line_height` shape and measure with them, applying `fs=`, `weight=`,
    `bold`, `semibold` and `italic` from the style string over the inherited font.
  * `gpui-binding/src/lib.rs` — the four bindings.
  * The `Arc` is not a raw pointer: `WindowTextSystem` is refcounted, so keeping
    one past the frame it came from is ordinary ownership, unlike `DISPATCH`.

The style semantics matter and are documented on `text_width`: **the family and
the size come from the window's inherited text style**, so `font=2xl` on an
ancestor is inherited by everything measured inside it, and `fs=` overrides the
size. A newline is flattened to a space first, because gpui's shaper panics on one —
the same thing `editor.rs` does to everything that reaches it.
