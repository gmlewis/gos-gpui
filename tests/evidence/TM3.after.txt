What makes it pass:

  * `gpui-binding/src/view.rs` — `highlight_style` reaches all seven of
    `HighlightStyle`'s properties, and the decorations carry their detail:
    `undercolor`, `underthick`, `underwavy`, `strikecolor`, `strikethick` and
    `fade`. A detail token **implies the decoration it belongs to**, so
    `underthick=3` is an underline — which is why the property list is a
    property list and not a token list.
  * `gpui-binding/src/view.rs` — `check_highlight` reports a token outside the
    vocabulary once, the way `check_style` reports an unknown style token, so a
    typo is a banner rather than a silence.
  * `gpui-binding/src/style.rs` — `is_known_highlight`, a vocabulary of its own.
    It is deliberately **not** the element one: sharing the table would let
    `p=16` through as a highlight, and the test says so.
  * `gpui-binding/src/lib.rs` — `gpui::highlight_properties(props)`, which
    answers what a spec sets as a comma-separated list in a fixed order.

Why this row's check is headless and not a GUI probe: what `text_styled` acts on
is the parse, and the parse is what can be wrong. Whether a colour reaches the
screen is a pixel question, and this machine cannot capture pixels — see "Pixel
verification is unavailable on this machine". A test of the parse is therefore as
strong as a screenshot would be for everything except the paint itself.
