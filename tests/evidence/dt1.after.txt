`h = 400.0` and `max = 219600.0` are the same in both runs, and they are worth a second look in
the *after* table: 400 + 219600 = 220000, which is exactly `10_000 * 22.0`, the row count times the
row height the port hands `ui::list`. So `max + h` is the content height, which is what lets the
port reach upstream's `scroll_height` — the one quantity in upstream's expression that the binding
has no name for — without an approximation.

The last line of the range is where the clamp earns its keep, and it is the one place where before
and after differ for a reason other than the denominator: upstream's `clamp` pins the thumb at
`h - 100 - 4 = 296`, and the rule the example carried had nothing to pin it, so it ran to 304 and
its own hundred pixels ended at 404 in a 400-pixel track. Both the comparison and the property
assertion fail there in the before-run, and both pass in the after-run.

The negative control is the sixth line, and it is the reason this check is not two copies of one
expression. `port_top` and `upstream_top` are separate transcriptions that happen to say the same
thing now; `was_top` is a third, saying what the example said before. It disagrees at 4 of the 5
points and not 5, which is the most a differing rule can disagree at — the top of the range has
percentage zero, so `4 + 0` and `clamp(0, 4, 296)` are both 4 — and the probe fails if it is not
exactly 4. Without that line, a green would say only that a function equals itself.
