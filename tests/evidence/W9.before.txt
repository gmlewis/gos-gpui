W9 — a second (and third) concurrent top-level window, without re-entering
`run`.
Tier: `gos run --main-thread`, and every other tier.

BEFORE THE WORK. Command, against a pristine copy of this project at HEAD
(`git archive HEAD` into /tmp/gpui-before):

    cd /tmp/gpui-before
    timeout -k 5 30 gos run --main-thread tests/gui/w9_three_windows.gos

Output: nothing, and exit 124 — the timeout killed it.

The probe fails its very first call: `gpui::window_id`, and then
`gpui::set_int_in`, do not exist in the pristine binding, so the view callback
is abandoned at its first line and the binding draws its error card instead.
That is the honest transcript, and it is the weaker kind of red: an unresolved
symbol, not the behaviour.

W9 is a composite row, and its behavioural half is the other rows'. Opening a
second and third top-level window already worked at HEAD — `examples/27_window`
does it — so what the row actually asks for is *two windows that do not corrupt
each other*, and that is exactly what W1 to W8 are. The evidence is theirs:

  * W3.before.txt — the child drains the parent's events, and the parent drains
    the child's.
  * W7.before.txt — the child's frame counter, `~keys` and `~root` all belong to
    the process, so the second window's frame is counted in the first's clock
    and it reads the first's keyboard setting.

Those two probes are also the ones that satisfy this row's own claim, because
they are two and three windows open at once in one `run` call, each with its own
view callback, neither re-entering anything. What W9 adds on top is the third
window, opened from inside the second window's frame, and the report it writes
back into window 1's state namespace with `gpui::set_int_in`.
