Output, identical on all five tiers (`check` prints `check: ok (1 items typed)`; each
of the other four exits 0 with no stderr but the toolchain duplicate-symbol link
warnings every build in this project prints):

    OK WH4 a child opened at x=7,y=9 with display=0 place=center landed at x=1130,y=620 (centre 1130,620), and reports place=center display=5

A *behavioural* check, and the only one in the WH block: the child is opened at x=7,
y=9 with `display=0 place=center`, and it lands at the display's centre — 1130, 620 on
this 2560x1440 display — rather than where it was told to go.

What is new: `gpui-binding/src/options.rs` reads `display=<index>`, resolves it to a
`DisplayId` through `cx.displays()`, and puts it in `WindowOptions::display_id`, which
is also what makes `place=center` centre on the *chosen* display rather than on the
primary one; and `place=center` now actually centres. `gpui::window_option("place")`
and `("display")` answer what the window was opened with.

The half that is *not* verified, and why: this machine has one display, so `display=0`
and saying nothing at all put the window in the same place, and no assertion here can
tell them apart. What the check does establish is that the token parses, reaches
`WindowOptions::display_id`, and names a real display — the child reports `display=5`,
which is the same id `gpui::display_field(0, "id")` reports. Whether a *second* display
would be honoured is unverified and is in the row's residual doubt.

A note on the probe's shape, because it cost a wrong transcript. `gpui::quit()` asks
the app to stop; it does **not** abort the callback that called it. Written as separate
`if` statements, this probe printed its FAIL line and then fell through and printed its
OK line in the same frame. The chain here is exclusive — every branch ends in a verdict
and the success line is the final `else` — and the failure below is one line.
