AFTER THE WORK. Command, in this tree:

    TIER_TIMEOUT=45 ./scripts/tiers.sh tests/gui/tm4_appearance.gos

Output, identical on all five tiers (`check` prints `check: ok (1 items typed)`;
each of the other four exits 0 with no stderr):

    OK TH1 TM4 the window reports its appearance, and every one of the eight default colours is that appearance's own

The two rows share one probe because they are one question: a default colour is
chosen from the window's appearance, so "the colours are right" cannot be answered
without knowing which appearance the window is in — and "the appearance is
reported" is worth nothing unless something depends on it.

What the probe does: the appearance must be `light` or `dark`; `gpui::colour_names()`
must be exactly the eight names; and **every one of the eight must equal what the
reported appearance says it should be**, compared exactly, through a second reader
that answers for either appearance.

What is new:

  * `gpui-binding/src/view.rs` — the window's appearance is captured per frame
    beside its text metrics, `appearance_of` folds gpui's two "vibrant" appearances
    into the plain ones they resemble, and `named_colors` is the single place that
    turns a `Colors` into eight named hex strings — used both by the frame and by
    the new reader.
  * `gpui-binding/src/lib.rs` — `gpui::appearance()` (TH1), `gpui::colour_names()`
    (the list a program had no way to enumerate) and `gpui::default_color_for(appearance,
    name)` (the other appearance's value, for a border or overlay that has to read
    against the theme the window is *not* using).

What the check would have caught, and is the reason to have it: a binding that
installed the light defaults and never corrected them fails on this machine — which
is in a dark appearance — with `background is #ffffffff while a dark appearance
says it should be #222222ff`. Before this round, none of the eight colours had ever
been compared against the appearance at all.
