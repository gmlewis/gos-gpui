The frame count varies between 15 and 16 across the tiers, which is why the assertion is
`>= 8`: it is a measurement of "the UI was painting", not of a frame rate. The two builds
print the duplicate-symbol `ld` warnings this project has carried since round 2.

What changed, all in `gpui-binding/src/`:

  * `lib.rs` — `gpui::task_result(id)`, the convention `"<id>.result"` with a name, so a
    program does not have to invent the key and a check can look at the result rather than at
    whatever key a program happened to choose. `gpui::task`'s doc now says what the work
    actually is and points at the compiler error that says why it cannot be a program's own
    code.
  * `README.md` — the `gpui::task` row, with `task_result` and the same point.
  * The tests: `tests/bg1_task_result_test.gos` (three headless cases) and
    `tests/gui/bg1_background.gos`, whose verdict is ordered so that each of its three claims
    has a break that reaches it and no other.

**This row needed no mechanism, and saying so is the point.** The handoff, the background
executor and the result channel were all in place; the register's "today: no" was stale in the
way TM4's, MR3's, TH4's and KM3's were. What the round added is the first **check** of the
claim the code's own comment makes, and the evidence that the one word that cannot be had —
*arbitrary* — cannot be had, from the compiler rather than from the obstacle column.

Residual doubt. **The result is a formatted string the binding composes**, not something the
work produced: `"<id> finished after <ms> ms"`. A program cannot hand a background job its own
data to bring back, and nothing here invents a job vocabulary to do it — that would duplicate
what Gossamer's own file I/O already does synchronously (BG7) and would be a guess at which
jobs matter. `gpui::task_result` cannot tell "not finished" from "never started", both being
`""`, which the doc says and which a program can disambiguate with an event that has not
arrived. The frame count is a wall-clock measurement in a 250ms window, so on a machine
loading badly enough to stop painting it could fall under the guard of 8 and fail rather than
hang — the same caveat BG4, BG5 and BG6 carry.
