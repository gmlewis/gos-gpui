MR4 — after: the platform's own click counter, reaching the program.

The row is "interactive-element event completeness: click variants, mouse up/move/exit,
`stop_propagation`" and this file is about its first clause. The platform has always known the
difference between a single, a double and a triple click: gpui's X11 client keeps
`state.current_count` and increments it when a press lands within `DOUBLE_CLICK_INTERVAL`
(400ms) of the last one, on the same button and within `DOUBLE_CLICK_DISTANCE` (5px) of the last
position (`gpui-0.2.2/src/platform/linux/x11/client.rs:1103-1125`,
`platform/linux/platform.rs:38-39`). `DW4`'s evidence has a double click taking a word and a
triple taking a line, so the count was reaching the editor all along. **A program could not
read it**: `gpui::on_mouse` raised `"<id>|<kind>"` and nothing else, and the number the platform
computed was discarded at the binding's edge.

--- three gestures, a real second apart, and why not "a few frames later"

400ms is *real* time and `gpui::animate()` runs frames about 16ms apart, so "a few frames later"
would be inside the window and every gesture would read as a continuation of the one before it.
`xdotool sleep 1 click ..` puts the wait in the helper, where it is real seconds and not frames,
and the leading `sleep 1` is what makes the first click a single even if a previous probe left a
click behind on the same display.

Each gesture is also tallied on its own — `g1d`, `g2d`, `g3d` and so on — rather than as one
total at the end. The three gestures post one, two and three clicks, so six presses in all is
what a run in which every click was heard says, and also what a run in which the first gesture
was heard three times says. The first version of this probe asked the total and was told `the
box heard 6 presses, not 3`.

--- the count arrives on the click as well as on the press

`<id>.count` is written from `MouseDownEvent::click_count` (`gpui-binding/src/view.rs:3102`),
`MouseUpEvent::click_count` (`:3114`) and `ClickEvent::Mouse(..).up.click_count` (`:3065`). The
third is the one that matters for a program: gpui synthesises a `ClickEvent` out of the pair and
a program listening for `click` should get the same number a program listening for `down` does,
not a default. `ClickEvent` has no `click_count` of its own — it carries the `MouseUpEvent` it
was made from — which is why the reading reaches through it rather than off it.

--- the environment finding this probe paid for, which is not about clicks

It failed the `tiers` run and passed standalone, twice, and the difference was 55 seconds. The
cause is `xdotool 3.20160805.1`'s `mousemove --sync`, which sits in its wait loop for its whole
retry budget — **17.820s, measured in isolation** — whenever the pointer is *already* at the
destination, and returns at once only when it had to move. Measured three ways: a plain
`mousemove 750 573` is instant and `getmouselocation` then reads exactly `x:750 y:573`; a
`mousemove --sync 750 573` straight after it takes 17.820s; and this probe's three gestures,
which all post to the same box centre, took 19075ms, 19105ms and 19182ms. This is the one probe
in the suite that returns to a point it has already been at, which is why it is the one that
found it — `dd2_drag_states.gos` syncs only to points it has just left and stays fast.

So the probe posts a plain `mousemove` and lets the real second that follows it be the
synchronisation. That is a better wait anyway: it waits for the program to have the pointer,
not for the server to agree that it does. The finding is written into `clicks()`'s own doc
comment, where the next person to reach for `--sync` will meet it.
