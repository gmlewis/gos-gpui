--- the three, and why each is a different mechanism

**The wheel.** `scroll` is `InteractiveElement::on_scroll_wheel` (`elements/div.rs:318`), gated
on `hitbox.should_handle_scroll(window)` and not on hover — the whole hit-test list, so an
element under the pointer *and* an ancestor that scrolls both hear it, which is why the kind
exists at both levels. The delta is not a number: it is a `ScrollDelta`, either `Lines(f32)` or
`Pixels(Pixels)`, and the binding writes whichever it got to `<id>.dx` / `<id>.dy` beside
`<id>.scroll_lines`, which says which of the two the slots hold. A program that assumed lines
would be wrong on a touchpad and right on `xdotool`; the flag is what lets it tell.

**Leaving an element.** gpui has no element-level exit event. An element's listeners are
registered as concrete types on mouse-down and its neighbours (`elements/div.rs:2042-2059`)
and `MouseExitEvent` is not among them. What an element does have is the **transition** of its
own hover, handed to the hover listener as a bool and evaluated only on a move. So the binding
keeps the last hover state per element and raises `"<id>|exit"` when it goes true→false — a
transition and not a state, which the probe checks by leaving once and reading one event, not
nine. This is also why the element-level exit can only arrive on a move: it has no event of its
own to carry a point, so the point it reports is the last one seen over the element.

**Leaving the window.** The same `MouseExitEvent`, generated by the platform
(`platform/linux/x11/client.rs:1217`) and dispatched to window listeners directly, so it
arrives whether or not the pointer ever moves again. That difference — one needs a move, one
does not — is why both are registered and why the probe drives them separately.

--- what the wheel's own failures say, and why the message is long

The first FAIL above is the honest one: if the wheel had not arrived, the probe could not tell
the binding's fault from XTEST's. The X11 backend drops a scroll button press flagged
`POINTER_EMULATED` (`platform/linux/x11/client.rs:1133-1135`) — the flag a smooth-scrolling
device's events carry beside their valuators, and the same filter GTK applies. `xdotool` posts
through XTEST and whether XTEST's events carry that flag is the X server's business. They do
not, on this image, and the wheel arrives as `Lines(3.0)`; the message stays because a reader
on an image where they did would otherwise get a red and no way to read it.

--- the other half

`+3.0` is `SCROLL_LINES` (`platform/linux/platform.rs:33`), and it is asserted as a number and
not as a sign: a probe that checked only the sign would pass for any delta at all, including
one in the wrong unit. The window-level move is the one kind this row did not have to add — it
came in with `DD4`'s `gpui::on_mouse_window` — and the probe re-checks it here, with the pointer
off the box, because a window-level listener that quietly stopped reporting once the pointer
left the element would still pass every other assertion in the file.
