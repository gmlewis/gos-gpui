The row's complaint is a picture of a scrollbar: `examples/02_data_table.gos`
draws a grey thumb at `top=4` over a `ui::list`, and its own header says why it is
inert —

  * No scroll handle exists. Upstream's thumb is *positioned* from the list's
    scroll state (`-scroll_top / scroll_height`) and *drags* through
    `UniformListScrollHandle::set_offset`. The binding exposes no such handle: a
    program can neither read the offset nor set it.

— and the two gaps are different in kind:

  * **Reading and writing a list's offset** was *unbuilt*, not impossible. gpui
    runs a `uniform_list`'s scrolling through a `ScrollHandle` kept inside the
    `UniformListScrollHandle`, hands that same handle to the inner scroller
    (`gpui-0.2.2/src/elements/uniform_list.rs:676`), and then shares the handle's
    offset *cell* with the list's own layout state outright
    (`uniform_list.rs:349`). So a list's offset, maximum and `set_offset` are the
    three operations a plain scrolled container already had — through the same
    type — and the whole of SC1 to SC3 applies to it once the reader looks in the
    right place.
  * **Capturing the drag** is a real limit, and a different row's: `gpui::on_mouse`
    reports while the pointer is over the element it was attached to and no
    further (`PORTING.md`, "Pointer capture is element-scoped"; DD4). That is not
    fixed here.
