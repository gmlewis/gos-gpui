CL2 — an unsaved-changes prompt on close, end to end.

BEFORE THE WORK.

**This row needed no binding code, and the red is therefore not one I can stage.** Its obstacle in the
register was "needs CL1 + CL4; nothing today", and both are done: CL1's `on_window_should_close`
(`lib.rs:1550`, tested in round 20) and CL4's queued platform sheet (tested in round 56). What is left
is the **flow** — that the two connect, with a person in the loop — so there is no new capability whose
absence could make a fresh check fail. Saying that plainly is better than staging a red for show.

**The reds this check composes are both recorded**, and they are worth naming because they are what
this row would have failed on:

  * **CL4's red** (`tests/evidence/CL4.before.txt`): a program queued two prompts in one frame and
    nothing appeared —

        CL4 queued two prompts in one frame: 1 and 2 — the first sheet should be up, the second waiting
        CL4 waiting, 0 of 2 answers so far, 26.70997 seconds in
        FAIL CL4 only 0 of 2 answers arrived — a sheet either never came up or was never answered

    That is CL2 failing at its step 3: a guard could refuse the close and have no way to ask why.

  * **CL1's red** (`tests/evidence/CL1.after.txt`): with every close allowed, the refusing child
    closed anyway —

        FAIL CL1 the refusing guard was ignored

    That is CL2 failing at its step 2: no veto, so nothing to ask about.

**Two things the first run of this probe got wrong, both of them the check rather than the code.**

  * **The verdict was printed after `gpui::run` returned, and never appeared.** stdout is block-buffered
    when it is a pipe, and the process goes away with the last window before anything flushes it — the
    trap this run's ledger has recorded since round 12, walked into anyway. The verdict now prints
    **inside the frame that allows the close**, while the window is still alive. What the missing lines
    would have proved — that the window really closed — is instead proved by the process ending *before*
    its bound: a probe that had never closed would have hit its 120-second FAIL and said so.
  * **The re-ask was unbounded.** On anything but Discard the probe asked again so Discard stayed one
    click away — reasonable, but with no limit: the owner pressed **Save** three times and had to click a
    fourth time to get out of a question they had already answered. It now re-asks **once**, then stops
    and says so.

That second one is worth recording as its own small lesson, because the owner had been promised "one
window, one click" and got four clicks — not from a bug, but from a probe that would not take an answer
for an answer.

  * **And the probe could not say which button had been pressed.** Its message for Save and for Cancel
    was the same sentence, so when this file was first written it recorded three **Cancel** clicks that
    were three **Save** clicks, and the owner had to correct it. A transcript a person has to be asked
    about is not evidence yet; the probe now prints the button's name.
