CL4 — a queued `window.prompt` with program-supplied buttons.

BEFORE THE WORK, and the red is a good one: **behavioural, not an unresolved symbol.** The queue
itself was built and tested in round 50 (`gpui::prompt` and `prompt_answer`), so the probe compiles,
queues two prompts in one frame, prints what the queue holds, and then… nothing appears. No sheet, no
answer, and the probe gives up on its own and says so:

    $ DSH_ALLOW_WINDOWS=1 ./scripts/run-once.sh tests/gui/cl4_prompt.gos 60
    window-guard: windows ALLOWED (DSH_ALLOW_WINDOWS=1)
    CL4 queued two prompts in one frame: 1 and 2 — the first sheet should be up, the second waiting
    CL4 the head is "1|CL4 — one of two|Click Alpha, Beta or Cancel." with buttons #["Alpha", "Beta", "Cancel"]
    CL4 waiting, 0 of 2 answers so far, 5.003068709 seconds in
    CL4 waiting, 0 of 2 answers so far, 26.70997 seconds in
    FAIL CL4 only 0 of 2 answers arrived — a sheet either never came up or was never answered
    no runners left

That is the row's obstacle in one transcript: a program can *ask* and nothing *shows*. The gap between
round 50's queue and this row is exactly the call to the platform.

**The first attempt at a check was narrower than it looked — for the tenth time in this run.** The
probe's patience was counted in **frames**, and the liveness lines above are the evidence against it:
5.0 seconds, then 26.7 — one frame every twenty-odd seconds. macOS throttles the animation frames of a
window that is not frontmost, so a frame-based patience expires eventually, or not at all, and a
frame-based *poll* would leave an answered sheet sitting there with the program's event arriving late.
Two changes followed, and both are in the finished code:

  * the probe's patience and its liveness line are counted in **seconds** (`gpui::now()`), not frames;
  * the binding collects the answer **on a 100 ms timer in a task**, not once a frame — so a click is
    picked up whether or not the window is being painted.

**The second attempt taught the other half of the same lesson.** The probe printed its verdict and
then printed it again on every frame that followed (`quit` still lets a few paint) — a verdict repeated
thirty times is noise, not evidence. The verdict now fires once, behind a `done` flag.

**And a third observation, which is why the round asked a human rather than guessing.** A run with the
sheet up and nobody clicking produced no verdict at all: the harness killed it at its bound with the
transcript ending at `CL4 a sheet came up for prompt.shown|1`. The window was raised, the sheet went up,
and whether anybody *saw* it is not something a transcript can say. The user was asked instead of
assumed — and it turned out they had moved on to other things, which is exactly the failure a
"self-terminating probe" cannot report about itself.
