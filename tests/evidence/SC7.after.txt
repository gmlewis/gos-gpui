SC7 — a custom scrollbar whose thumb drags the real offset.

AFTER THE WORK. Command, in this tree:

    TIER_TIMEOUT=45 ./scripts/tiers.sh tests/gui/sc7_scrollbar.gos

Output, identical on all five tiers (`check` prints `check: ok (1 items typed)`;
each of the other four exits 0 with no stderr):

    OK SC7 the list's offset and maximum are readable, and setting the offset the way a thumb would moves its rows

What the probe does: a 200-row virtual list in a 200-pixel viewport, and the three
numbers a scrollbar is arithmetic over. At frame 2 it requires a positive maximum
(the track has a length) and offset 0 (the thumb starts at the top, not at a
guess). At frame 5 it sets the offset to -2000 — what a thumb dragged to the
middle of its track asks for — and at frame 8 it requires both that the offset
reads back and that the rows moved: `list_range` must start at row 100.

What makes it pass:

  * `gpui-binding/src/view.rs` — `scroll_offset`, `scroll_max_offset`,
    `scroll_bounds` and `scroll_to` resolve `id` through one `scrolled()` helper:
    a scrolled container's own handle, or a virtual list's base handle.
  * `gpui-binding/src/list.rs` — `base_handle(win, id)`, the plain `ScrollHandle`
    inside a `UniformListScrollHandle`.
  * `gpui-binding/src/lib.rs` — `gpui::scroll_bounds(id, field)`, new here: the
    container's own box, which is what a thumb is *sized* from. Reading the
    offset, the maximum and the viewport are the three numbers a scrollbar needs,
    and only the first two existed.
  * `examples/02_data_table.gos` — the thumb is no longer inert. It is positioned
    from `-scroll_offset / scroll_max_offset` over a track of
    `scroll_bounds("items", "h") - THUMB_HEIGHT`, exactly upstream's
    `-scroll_top / scroll_height`, and a drag of it calls `gpui::scroll_to`.
    Because `on_mouse` is element-scoped, the *strip* at the right edge listens
    rather than the 8x100 thumb: a vertical drag stays inside the strip for its
    whole length, and the strip is narrow enough that the wheel over the body
    still reaches the list. The example's header records both that and the
    deviation that remains.

Regression: `./scripts/regress.sh` after the example change — 27 examples, silent.
`examples/02_data_table.gos` itself, run under `scripts/run-once.sh`: exit 124 with
**no output**, which for an interactive example that never quits is the pass
condition.

What is not verified, and why it is in residual doubt rather than claimed: the
drag *input* path. Nothing in the binding synthesizes a pointer event, so the
probe drives the offset with `scroll_to` — everything a drag would act on — and
the drag itself is exercised only by a person with a mouse.

THE PIXEL CHECK, ATTEMPTED AND NOT OBTAINED. This row's evidence is partly
pixels, so a temporary probe was written to put a fixed frame on screen: a
200-pixel list, a cyan 40x2 marker at the container's top, and a magenta 20x40
thumb at a computed `top`. The probe printed, from a real layout,

    visual: offset=-1900 max=3800 view=200 thumb_top=80

so the three numbers and the arithmetic are right in a laid-out window and not
only in the probe's own synthetic case — that much is evidence, and it is worth
keeping.

The capture is not. `screencapture -x /tmp/thumb.png` taken twelve seconds into
the run, then analysed by colour-component search (this model cannot read images
directly), found no component of the marker's or the thumb's size or shape
anywhere on the 2560x1440 main display. The largest magenta component was 5x18
pixels and the largest cyan one 45x6, at unrelated positions — screen noise, not
the probe. **The window was therefore not on the captured display**, most likely
behind whatever had focus, and no conclusion about how the thumb *renders* can be
drawn from it. `tools/upstream-examples.sh run data_table` was not run either, so
there is no comparison artifact.

Recorded rather than papered over: the scrollbar's geometry is verified by
numbers, its rendering is not verified at all.
