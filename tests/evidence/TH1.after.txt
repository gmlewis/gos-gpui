`gpui::appearance()` answers `"light"` or `"dark"`, asked of the window rather than
assumed. gpui's `WindowAppearance` has four variants — `Light`, `Dark`,
`VibrantLight`, `VibrantDark` — and the two vibrant ones are reported as the plain
ones they resemble, because **no colour in `gpui::colors::Colors` distinguishes
them**: `for_appearance` itself maps `VibrantLight` to the light set and
`VibrantDark` to the dark one (`colors.rs:36-41`). Two words are the whole of what
this binding can honestly say, and the doc says so rather than offering four names
that collapse.

The check has teeth: the appearance is not merely asserted to be one of two words,
it is the thing the eight default colours are then compared *against*. A wrong
appearance answer and a wrong colour set cannot both be right — they are checked
against each other, so the pair is worth more than either alone.
