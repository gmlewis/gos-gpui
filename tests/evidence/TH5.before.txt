**Two of five, and the other three are worth reading as a finding rather than as a
gap.** `unknown_tokens` only ever looked at a token's *key*; nothing in the language
validated a *value*, so `bg=theme:nosuch` and `bg=theme:` were both accepted and the two
tests that require them to be refused are the two that failed. The three that passed did
so for the same reason and at the same time: a value nothing checks is a value anything
satisfies, including the good ones. That is why the red for "the vocabulary" is a red
about refusal and not about acceptance.

**2. The window half.**

    $ timeout -k 5 60 gos run --main-thread tests/gui/th5_theme_tokens.gos
    exit 124, no output

The silent hang: `gpui::resolve_colour` does not exist at HEAD, so the callback is
abandoned at its first call and the window idles — the shape KM5's, KM6's, KM2's and
KM3's transcripts have, and the third time in this run that an unbound binding function
has produced it. The verdict is block-buffered and vanishes with the kill, which is why
the transcript is empty rather than truncated.

**The absence itself, stated plainly.** `gpui::colour_names`, `gpui::default_color`,
`gpui::default_color_for`, `theme.<field>=` (TH4) and `gpui::set_theme` (TH3) all existed;
what did not is any way for a *style string* to name the table. A program that wanted the
desktop's own background had to read `default_color("background")` and paste the hex into
its style string, and paste a second one for the other scheme — which is the whole reason
a `theme:` value is worth having.
