### The red, seen rather than cited

The row's original red is the text arithmetic DW1 carries (`seven × error[GX0002]: name
gpui::text_lines …`), which is the half a click stands on and not the click. The click's own
red was staged: the widget's one line of click-count handling,

    let range = match event.click_count {   →   let range = match 1 {

so that a click is always a click and never a word or a line — the state upstream's `TextInput`
is in. The same probe, the same window, the same pointer, then said:

    DW4 two clicks: selection 21,21, the word at 21 is 18,24
    FAIL DW4 two clicks selected 21,21, and the word the caret 21 is in is 18,24
    DW4 three clicks: selection 21,21, the line at 21 is 18,29
    FAIL DW4 three clicks selected 21,21, and the line the caret 21 is on is 18,29
    DW4 three clicks at the top of the box: selection 4,4, the first line is 0,17
    FAIL DW4 three clicks at the top of the box selected 4,4, and the first line is 0,17

Three reds and no others: the single click and the drag do not depend on the count, and the
autoscroll clause has nothing to do with it. Restoring the line and re-running gives the
transcript above, three `FAIL`s less. The check is therefore sensitive to the thing the row is
about, and not only to the arithmetic underneath it.

### Why the click has to come from outside

`Window::dispatch_event` is public (`gpui-0.2.2/src/window.rs:3581`) and takes a
`PlatformInput`, which is public too (`interactive.rs:547`). It cannot be called: its
return type, `DispatchEventResult`, is `pub(crate)` (`window.rs:1273`), and a public
function whose return type is private is not callable from another crate however it is
wrapped. A two-crate experiment settled it rather than the citation — a direct call, a
discarded result (`let _ = …`) and a generic-erasure wrapper (`ignore(w.dispatch(3))`)
all fail with `error: type a::Result_ is private`. Nothing else on `Window` injects
input: `dispatch_action`, `dispatch_keystroke` and `handle_input` are actions and keys,
and `on_mouse_event` only registers the binding's own observer.

So the input comes from the operating system. **`scripts/pointer.sh`** is a new tool: a
small Swift program that posts `CGEvent`s at the window server's event tap (compiled on
demand into `~/.cache/learn-gpui/pointer`, never into this tree). It refuses to run at
all unless `DSH_ALLOW_WINDOWS=1` is set, because it moves the *real* pointer and its
click lands in whatever window is under it. It offers `pos`, `move`, `click x y
[count]`, `drag x1 y1 x2 y2 [steps]`, `scroll`, `windows`, `frame <pid>` and
`owner x y`.

### What makes the check honest

**The reader.** `gpui::editor_selection(key)` (and `…_in`, per window) is new: what the
editor has selected, as `"<start>,<end>"` in bytes, read back from the widget the way
`gpui::editor_text` and `gpui::editor_chrome` are. Text alone could not tell a caret
from a word from a line — all three leave the same characters behind — which is why this
row could not be checked before it existed. `gpui::editor_bounds(key, field)` comes with
it: the box the editor was last drawn in, in the window's own coordinates, so a program
can aim something at its own widget.

**The expectations are asked of the crate, not written down.** The double-click's
expected range is `gpui::text_word(content, caret)` and the triple-click's is
`gpui::text_line_bounds(content, caret)` — at the caret the platform's *own* click
produced, read from the selection step 1 left behind. The click picks a byte; the
arithmetic DW1 already checks says what that byte means. Writing `18,24` into the probe
would have made it a test of the font metrics, and it would break the first time the
window's font changed. The probe does insist the caret landed strictly *inside* the
word, so a badly-aimed click is reported as a badly-aimed click rather than passing.

**Nothing is posted at someone else's window.** Before the first click the probe asks
`scripts/pointer.sh owner <x> <y>` for the pid of the frontmost window under the point and
requires it to be its own process — `28286` in the transcript above. A click that
landed elsewhere would otherwise look exactly like a widget that ignores the mouse. That
check also caught a real mistake during this round: aiming at `inner_y` misses by the
height of the titlebar, and the aim had to be measured from the window server instead
(see §3).

**Three presses, not one press with a count.** `click x y 2` posts two down/up pairs
with the platform's own click counter set to 1 then 2, which is what a hand does; the
widget reads that counter back as `click_count`.

## 3. What else this round found

**`gpui::window_field("inner_y")` is not the content's origin on this platform.** The
probe asks for a window 300 pixels tall and `gpui::window_field("h")` answers 332; the
window server agrees — `1060 570 440 332` — so 32 pixels of it are the titlebar, and the
content starts at y=602, not at the frame's 570. `inner_x`, `inner_y`, `inner_w` and
`inner_h` answer the frame's numbers (which is what WH6 records: "outer 320x232 and inner
320x232 while windowed"). A program aiming at its own widget must add the decoration the
window asked for and did not get: `frame height − asked height`. Getting this wrong is
not a crash — it aims one row higher than intended, which is precisely the wrong answer a
click test must not have, so the probe measures the window server's rectangle rather than
trusting `inner_y`.

**`Window::request_autoscroll` has no consumer but gpui's `List`.** It is defined at
`gpui-0.2.2/src/window.rs:2484` and `take_autoscroll` at `:2491`, and the only caller of
either is `elements/list.rs:808` and `:819`. A leaf element's request is dropped.
Upstream's `TextInput` — what this editor is a port of
(`tools/upstream/examples/input.rs`) — has no autoscroll either, and this editor has no
viewport of its own: it draws its whole document in its box and maps a point to a row by
dividing by the line height. So a drag has nothing to scroll, and building one would be
building a scrolling editor viewport, which is a different capability and not this row.

**What the probe measures instead.** A second editor of twelve lines sits in a scroll
panel forty pixels tall. The pointer is pressed inside the panel and dragged to 190 —
past the panel's own bottom edge, which is exactly where an autoscroll would have to
happen. The panel's offset reads `0` before and `0` after: nothing scrolled, and the drag
that left the box selected nothing further. That is this clause's answer — **unreachable,
with the reason named** — rather than an assertion that a scroll happened, which no
implementation in this crate could satisfy.
