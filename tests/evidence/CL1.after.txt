CL1 — intercept and veto a window close (`on_window_should_close`).

AFTER THE WORK. Command, in this tree:

    TIER_TIMEOUT=45 ./scripts/tiers.sh tests/gui/cl1_should_close.gos

Output, identical on all five tiers (`check` prints `check: ok (1 items typed)`;
each of the other four exits 0 with no stderr):

    OK CL1 the refusing guard held its window open and the agreeing one let another close

What the probe does: window 1 opens two children. Window 2 installs a guard that
answers `false`; window 3 installs one that answers `true`. Window 1 closes **both
by name** at frame 6, then samples each child's self-report at frame 8 and again at
frame 12. The refusing child must still be reporting — `now != was` — and the
agreeing one must have stopped — `now == was`. A sample is only meaningful if there
was a first, which the probe also requires, so a child that never rendered cannot
pass by being absent.

What makes it pass:

  * `gpui-binding/src/view.rs` — a per-window registry of should-close handlers,
    `take_should_close_for_platform` handing one to the platform exactly once
    (gpui's `on_window_should_close` replaces whatever came before it), and
    `may_close` / `ask_should_close` consulting it. A handler that fails answers
    `true`, as gpui treats its own (`unwrap_or(true)`) — a broken guard must not
    trap a window forever.
  * `gpui-binding/src/app.rs` — the handler goes to the platform in
    `process_requests`, where there is a `Window` to give it to; and both
    `Request::CloseWindow` and `Request::CloseWindowAt` ask `may_close` before
    removing anything.
  * `gpui-binding/src/lib.rs` — `on_window_should_close(decide)`.

Regression: `./scripts/regress.sh` after the change — 27 examples, silent. That
matters here more than for most rows: `close_window` and `close_window_in` are used
by `examples/10_on_window_close_quit` and `examples/27_window`, and both now consult
a handler. Neither installs one, so `may_close` answers `true` unchanged — which is
exactly what the silent suite confirms.
